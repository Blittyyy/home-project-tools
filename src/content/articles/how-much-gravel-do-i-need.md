**Gravel volume** =

**area × depth**

For a rectangle:

**area = length × width**

Convert depth to feet (inches ÷ 12), multiply for **cubic feet**, then divide by **27** for **cubic yards**.

Estimated weight comes next: multiply volume by a **bulk density** in consistent units (for example lb/ft³ → pounds, then ÷ 2,000 for US short tons).

A common mistake is treating one weight-per-yard number as universal for every gravel product.

## Measure the project area

- **Length** and **width** for rectangles
- **Diameter** for circles
- A known **area** when the shape is irregular but you already have square footage

Break L-shaped or curved beds into simpler pieces and add the areas. The FixUpWise [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator) supports rectangle, circle, and known-area modes.

## Convert depth into feet

Depth must be in the same length system as the area before you multiply.

**Depth in feet = depth in inches ÷ 12**

Example: **3 in** → **0.25 ft**

Enter the depth you plan to place — the calculator does not choose depth for you.

## Calculate cubic feet and cubic yards

1. Cubic feet = area (sq ft) × depth (ft)  
2. Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ **27**

Example: **10 × 10 ft** at **2 in** (geometric volume only — **0%** extra material)

- Area = **100 sq ft**
- Depth = 2 ÷ 12 ≈ **0.1667 ft**
- Volume ≈ **16.67 cu ft** ≈ **0.62 yd³** before extra material

To reproduce that yardage in the [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator), set Extra material to **0%** (the tool defaults to **10%**). More fixed-size walkthroughs: [how much gravel for a 10×10 area](/projects/how-much-gravel-for-10x10-area).

## Convert volume into estimated weight

Weight is not the same as volume.

**Pounds ≈ cubic feet × density (lb/ft³)**  
**US short tons ≈ pounds ÷ 2,000**

The calculator’s planning presets follow Caterpillar loose-material figures:

| Planning material | Density used | Approx. US short tons / yd³ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Crushed stone (loose) | 100 lb/ft³ (2,700 lb/yd³) | ≈ 1.35 |
| Dry gravel, ¼–2 in (loose) | ≈ 105.6 lb/ft³ (2,850 lb/yd³) | ≈ 1.43 |

Those are estimating defaults. Caterpillar notes that weight varies with moisture, grain size, and compaction — and that tests are needed for exact characteristics. Prefer a density from your supplier when you have one. Full reference: [how much a yard of gravel weighs](/materials/how-much-does-a-yard-of-gravel-weigh).

## Irregular areas and optional extra material

Uneven grade, soft spots, and curves often take more material than a flat drawing suggests. The calculator’s extra-material field defaults to **10%** as an editable planning allowance (0%, 5%, 10%, or custom up to 25%). Geometric examples in this guide omit that allowance — choose **0%** in the calculator to match them. The percentage is **not** a verified compaction model and not a universal requirement.

## Ordering by cubic yard versus ton

Some yards quote by the **cubic yard**; many truck deliveries are sold by the **ton**. Convert only after you know which unit the quote uses. Coverage from a stated tonnage is a different question — see [how much area a ton of gravel covers](/materials/how-much-area-does-a-ton-of-gravel-cover).

## Pre-purchase checklist

- Project measured (or known area entered)
- Intended depth chosen for this layer
- Whether the supplier sells by yard or ton confirmed
- Density or tons-per-yard figure confirmed when buying by weight
- Optional extra-material percentage chosen deliberately (including 0%)

Pea gravel and decorative products follow the same volume math; weight still needs a product-specific density — see [how much pea gravel do I need](/materials/how-much-pea-gravel-do-i-need). Driveway jobs often need **separate layer estimates** — see [how much gravel for a driveway](/projects/how-much-gravel-for-a-driveway).
