A driveway gravel estimate starts the same way as any layer:

**volume = length × width × depth**

What makes driveways different is **which layer** you are buying for — a thin **resurface** on an existing drive is not the same shopping list as a **new** section that needs base and surface materials.

  <p class="planning-note__title">Calculate each layer separately</p>
  <p class="planning-note__body">New construction can require different products and thicknesses for base and surface. Estimate each layer on its own dimensions and depth, then convert each volume to weight with the density that matches that product. ## Measuring driveway length and width

Measure the traveled surface you intend to cover:

- **Length** along the drive  
- **Width** across the drive (include flared entrances as separate rectangles if needed)  
- Note soft edges, crown, and drainage that change how deep material settles  

Example footprint used below: **12 × 50 ft** = **600 sq ft**. Replace those numbers with your measurements.

## Resurfacing versus new construction

**Resurfacing** usually means adding a wearing layer on an existing gravel drive — one material, one depth decision, and a check that the existing base is still sound.

**New construction** may need:

- Subgrade preparation and drainage  
- One or more **base** layers  
- A separate **surface** gravel that binds differently than clean base stone  

FHWA’s gravel-roads guidance emphasizes that good **surface** gravel is not the same product as many **base** aggregates. Do not assume one stockpile serves both jobs.

Published design manuals sometimes cite example thicknesses (for instance about **4–6 in** of surfacing material in some low-volume road guidance, or jurisdiction driveway notes that mention about **6 in** of gravel for certain unpaved entrances). Those are **context-specific references**, not a FixUpWise recommendation for your driveway.

## Estimating layers separately

For each layer:

1. Enter length, width, and that layer’s depth  
2. Compute cubic yards  
3. Convert to tons with that product’s density  
4. Add only the extra-material percentage you intend for that layer  

Run the [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator) once per layer rather than averaging depths.

## Volume-to-weight conversion

**Tons ≈ (cubic yards × lb per cubic yard) ÷ 2,000**

Or multiply cubic feet by lb/ft³, then divide by 2,000. Keep **US short tons** (2,000 lb) labeled when comparing supplier tickets.

## Compaction and ordering considerations

Compaction changes finished thickness versus loose delivery volume. Road-base products may need supplier or engineer guidance for **loose order quantity versus compacted depth**. Do not treat the calculator’s percentage field as that model.

Order rounding, truck minimums, and moisture also move the final ticket.

## What to discuss with the aggregate supplier

- Resurface versus full rebuild  
- Separate base and surface products if needed  
- Gradation / fines (binding surface gravel vs clean base)  
- Sold by ton or yard, and their conversion factor  
- Delivery access, minimum load, and how they round partial tons
