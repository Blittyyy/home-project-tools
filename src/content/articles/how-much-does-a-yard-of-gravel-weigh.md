**A cubic yard is a unit of volume — not weight.**

**1 cubic yard = 27 cubic feet**, always.

How heavy that yard is depends on **bulk density** (how much mass fits in that loose volume). Moisture, grain size, rock type, and compaction all move the number. Caterpillar’s earthwork table is explicit: tests are needed for exact material characteristics.

## Bulk density versus solid rock

**Bulk density** includes the voids between particles in a loose (or bank) state. It is lower than the density of a solid piece of the same rock. Ordering math uses bulk density — the same idea behind the FixUpWise [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator) weight estimate.

Caterpillar also distinguishes:

- **Loose** cubic yards (excavated / swollen material)  
- **Bank** cubic yards (in-place natural state)  
- **Compact** cubic yards (after compaction)

The planning presets in FixUpWise use **loose** figures.

## Verified material examples (loose)

From Caterpillar’s published loose weights:

| Material (Caterpillar description) | Loose bulk density | Approx. US short tons / yd³ | Source |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Crushed stone | 2,700 lb/yd³ (100 lb/ft³) | 1.35 | Caterpillar earthwork table |
| Dry gravel, ¼–2 in | 2,850 lb/yd³ (≈ 105.6 lb/ft³) | ≈ 1.43 | Caterpillar earthwork table |
| Dry gravel (general row) | 2,550 lb/yd³ | ≈ 1.28 | Caterpillar earthwork table |
| Wet gravel, ¼–2 in | 3,400 lb/yd³ | 1.70 | Caterpillar earthwork table |
| Pit-run gravel | 3,250 lb/yd³ | ≈ 1.63 | Caterpillar earthwork table |

FixUpWise calculator presets use the **crushed stone** and **dry gravel ¼–2 in** rows. They are estimating defaults, not supplier guarantees.

## Cubic yards to US short tons

**1 US short ton = 2,000 lb**

**US short tons ≈ (lb per cubic yard × cubic yards) ÷ 2,000**

Or:

**US short tons ≈ (density lb/ft³ × cubic feet) ÷ 2,000**

Keep “US short ton” labeled when comparing metric tonnes (about 2,204.6 lb).

## Why supplier estimates differ

Quarries measure different products, moisture conditions, and rounding rules. Pea gravel tickets often land near **2,800–3,000 lb/yd³** in supplier literature — close to some Caterpillar gravel rows, but still product-specific. See [how much pea gravel do I need](/materials/how-much-pea-gravel-do-i-need).

## Worked conversion example

**2.00 cubic yards** of loose crushed stone at **2,700 lb/yd³**:

- Weight = 2.00 × 2,700 = **5,400 lb**  
- US short tons = 5,400 ÷ 2,000 = **2.70 tons**

Same volume of loose dry gravel ¼–2 in at **2,850 lb/yd³**:

- Weight = 2.00 × 2,850 = **5,700 lb** = **2.85 US short tons**

Same yards, different tons — because density changed.
