**Pea gravel volume** uses the same geometry as other gravel:

**cubic feet = area × depth in feet**  
**cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27**

What changes is the **product**: small, smooth, rounded stone (often sold around **⅜ in**), usually placed as a loose surface on paths, patios, and beds — not as a universal driveway wearing course.

## What makes pea gravel different

- **Shape:** rounded and smooth rather than angular crushed faces  
- **Feel:** comfortable underfoot for casual paths and seating areas  
- **Stability:** remains movable; chair legs and soft spots show quickly if the layer is too deep or the base is weak  
- **Purchase:** often sold by the **ton**, with yards converted using a yard-specific factor  

Angular crushed stone packs and interlocks differently. Treat pea gravel as its own product when you ask the yard for density and coverage.

## Measuring patios, walkways, and beds

1. Measure **length × width** (or enter known area)  
2. Choose the **finish depth** for that use  
3. Convert depth to feet and compute volume  
4. Convert to tons only with a density you trust  

Example depths below are **use-context figures from published sources**, not universal rules:

| Use context | Depth figure cited | Source context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inorganic mulch / stone dressing | about **1 in** | University of Maryland Extension mulching guidance |
| Casual patio / seating surface | about **2 in** over a compacted base | Supplier patio guidance (e.g. Gravel Valet) |
| Walkways / beds (working depth) | about **2 in** (deeper can feel loose) | Supplier product notes (e.g. Southland) |

Confirm drainage, edging, and base requirements for your site. Local code and soil conditions can change what belongs under the pebbles.

## Example quantity calculation

Volumes below are **geometric only** (no extra-material allowance). In the [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator), set Extra material to **0%** to match them — the tool defaults to **10%**.

**100 sq ft patio** at **2 in** (illustrative depth):

- Depth = 2 ÷ 12 ≈ **0.1667 ft**  
- Volume = 100 × 0.1667 ≈ **16.67 cu ft** ≈ **0.62 yd³** before extra material  

**3 × 20 ft walkway** at **3 in** (illustrative depth):

- Area = **60 sq ft**  
- Volume = 60 × 0.25 = **15.00 cu ft** ≈ **0.56 yd³** before extra material  

Same 100 sq ft footprint at other depths: [how much gravel for a 10×10 area](/projects/how-much-gravel-for-10x10-area).

## Coverage at different depths

Coverage shrinks as depth rises. One cubic yard covers:

**sq ft ≈ 27 ÷ depth in feet**

| Depth | Approx. sq ft per cubic yard |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 in | ≈ 324 |
| 2 in | ≈ 162 |
| 3 in | ≈ 108 |
| 4 in | ≈ 81 |

These are geometric coverage figures, not installation recommendations.

## Volume versus weight when purchasing pea gravel

Supplier published planning weights for pea gravel often land near **2,800–3,000 lb per cubic yard** (about **1.4–1.5 US short tons/yd³**), and some quote about **2,850 lb/yd³**. That happens to match Caterpillar’s loose **dry gravel, ¼–2 in** row (**2,850 lb/yd³**) — useful as a **planning proxy**, not proof that every pea gravel product equals that table row.

Using the calculator’s **Dry gravel, ¼–2 in (loose)** preset — **2,850 lb/yd³** (≈ **105.6 lb/ft³**) — as a **labeled planning proxy** on the **100 sq ft / 2 in** geometric volume:

- ≈ **16.67 cu ft × 105.6 ≈ 1,760 lb** ≈ **0.88 US short tons** before extra material (Extra material **0%**)

If your ticket says a different tons-per-yard factor, use **Custom density** instead. Broader density context: [how much a yard of gravel weighs](/materials/how-much-does-a-yard-of-gravel-weigh).

## What to confirm with the supplier

- Sold by **ton** or by **yard**  
- Their **lb/yd³** or tons-per-yard conversion for this product  
- Whether color/size blends change between loads  
- Delivery minimums and how they round partial tons
