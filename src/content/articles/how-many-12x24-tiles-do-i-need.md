A **12 × 24 in** tile has a face area of:

**12 × 24 = 288 sq in**  
**288 ÷ 144 = 2 sq ft**

So:

**raw tile count = project square footage ÷ 2**  
**tiles to buy = ceil(raw × (1 + waste%))**

For 12×24 tile, dividing the floor area by 2 is the easy part. Layout and cuts determine how much the raw count needs to grow.

## Quick-reference table (10% extra)

| Project area | Raw 12×24 | At 10% |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 sq ft | 50 | **55** |
| 120 sq ft | 60 | **66** |
| 200 sq ft | 100 | **110** |

These match the [Tile Calculator](/calculators/tile-calculator) with one final ceiling.

## Waste-adjusted examples

At **0%**: 100 sq ft → **50** tiles.  
At **10%**: **55**.  
At **15%** (common diagonal planning band): ceil(50 × 1.15) = **58**.

Raise the allowance for diagonal layouts or heavy cutting — 10% is a straight-layout planning default, not a requirement. See [how much extra tile should you buy?](/guides/how-much-extra-tile-should-you-buy).

## Boxes depend on packaging

Convert after the purchase count. If a carton lists tiles per box or coverage per box, use that figure. Example face math: 8 tiles × 2 sq ft = **16 sq ft** nominal — the label still controls. See [square feet in a box of tile](/materials/how-many-square-feet-in-a-box-of-tile).

## Large-format cuts

12×24 is large enough that awkward perimeter pieces and openings can increase unused offcuts. That does not change the face-area formula; it can change the allowance you choose. Full process: [how much tile do I need?](/projects/how-much-tile-do-i-need).
