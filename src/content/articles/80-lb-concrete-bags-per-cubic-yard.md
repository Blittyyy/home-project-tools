Bagged concrete is sold by weight, but you still plan volume in cubic feet or cubic yards. The bridge between them is the bag’s **approximate yield**.

## The 80-lb math

The FixUpWise Concrete Calculator uses approximate yields published for **QUIKRETE Concrete Mix #1101**:

- **80 lb ≈ 0.60 ft³ per bag**

One cubic yard is **27 cubic feet**, so:

**27 ÷ 0.60 = 45 bags per cubic yard**

That is the **theoretical yield** for this product: how many bags would fill one cubic yard if every bag delivered exactly 0.60 ft³ and you needed no contingency.

## Theoretical yield vs project bag counts

For a real project, the calculator:

1. Computes purchase volume (calculated volume plus any extra-material percent you choose).
2. Divides by the bag yield.
3. Rounds **up** to whole bags.

So a job that is not exactly one cubic yard will not land on exactly 45 bags, and a 10% allowance increases the count further. Example: a [10×10 slab at 4 in](/projects/how-much-concrete-for-10x10-slab) is about 1.23 yd³ before extra — **56** of the 80-lb bags at 0% extra, or **62** with a 10% allowance.

These figures apply to this product line’s published approximate yields. **Other brands and mixes can differ.** Always confirm the yield printed on the bag or data sheet for the product you buy.

## Bags or ready-mix?

Comparing cubic yards to bag counts helps you decide whether a bagged pour is practical. Larger volumes are often ordered as ready-mix, subject to supplier minimums and delivery fees. The calculator does not set a universal cutoff between bags and a truck.
