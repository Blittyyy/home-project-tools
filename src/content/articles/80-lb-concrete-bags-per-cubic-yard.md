Using the **0.60-cu-ft** yield published for an **80-lb bag of QUIKRETE Concrete Mix #1101**, one cubic yard works out to **45 bags**.

That number is product-specific. Check the yield printed for the mix you are actually buying.

Bag weight alone is not enough to estimate quantity; **yield** is the number that matters.

## Why 45 bags is a yield calculation

One cubic yard is **27 cubic feet**.

For the 80-lb QUIKRETE #1101 yield used by the FixUpWise calculator:

**27 ÷ 0.60 = 45 bags**

That is how many bags would fill one cubic yard if each bag delivered exactly 0.60 ft³. It is not a brand-agnostic rule for every 80-lb concrete mix.

## Product-specific yield

Bagged concrete is sold by weight. Volume planning still needs the approximate yield printed for that product.

The FixUpWise [Concrete Calculator](/calculators/concrete-calculator) uses the QUIKRETE Concrete Mix **#1101** approximate yields. Other mixes — even other 80-lb bags — can list a different yield.

  <p class="planning-note__body">An 80-lb bag from another product line may not yield 0.60 cu ft. Use the figure on the bag or data sheet for the mix you buy, then divide your cubic-foot volume by that yield.</p>

## What to check on the bag

Before you multiply:

- Product name / mix number
- Bag weight
- **Approximate yield** (cu ft or equivalent)
- Any note that yield is approximate or depends on mixing

If the printed yield differs from 0.60 ft³, replace that figure in the division. Do not assume every 80-lb bag matches #1101.

## Whole-bag rounding

Projects rarely need exactly one cubic yard.

The calculator:

1. Computes volume from your dimensions
2. Applies any extra-material percent you choose
3. Divides by bag yield
4. Rounds **up** to whole bags

Example: a [10×10 slab at 4 in](/projects/how-much-concrete-for-10x10-slab) is about **1.23 yd³** before extra — **56** of the 80-lb #1101 bags at 0% extra, or **62** with a 10% allowance.

Larger volumes are often ordered as ready-mix instead of bags; the calculator does not set a universal cutoff. For the volume formula, see [how to calculate cubic yards of concrete](/guides/how-to-calculate-cubic-yards-concrete).
