A **10 × 10 ft** area is **100 square feet**.

**Before extra material** (geometric volume only). To reproduce these yards in the [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator), set Extra material to **0%** — the tool defaults to **10%**.

| Depth | Cubic feet | Cubic yards |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2 inches | 16.67 | ≈ 0.62 |
| 3 inches | 25.00 | ≈ 0.93 |
| 4 inches | 33.33 | ≈ 1.23 |
| 6 inches | 50.00 | ≈ 1.85 |

A common mistake is quoting one gravel quantity for a 10×10 without stating the intended depth.

<aside class="planning-note" role="note">
  <p class="planning-note__title">Footprint alone is not enough</p>
  <p class="planning-note__body">100 square feet tells you the area. Depth finishes the volume. Weight still needs a bulk-density assumption after that.</p>
</aside>

## The 100-square-foot calculation

**Area = 10 × 10 = 100 sq ft**

**Cubic feet = 100 × (depth inches ÷ 12)**  
**Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27**

Intermediate values above use full precision before rounding the displayed yards.

## Worked example at 3 inches

- Depth = 3 ÷ 12 = **0.25 ft**  
- Volume = 100 × 0.25 = **25.00 cu ft**  
- Yards = 25 ÷ 27 ≈ **0.93 yd³** before extra material  

With the calculator’s default **10%** extra: purchase volume ≈ **1.02 yd³**.

## Estimated weight at a stated density

Using the FixUpWise **Crushed stone (loose, planning)** preset — **100 lb/ft³** (Caterpillar loose crushed stone **2,700 lb/yd³**) — on the same geometric volumes (Extra material **0%**):

| Depth | Approx. US short tons (before extra) |
| --- | ---: |
| 2 in | ≈ 0.83 |
| 3 in | 1.25 |
| 4 in | ≈ 1.67 |
| 6 in | 2.50 |

Switch to **Dry gravel, ¼–2 in (loose)** (**≈ 105.6 lb/ft³**) and the same volumes weigh about **5.6%** more. Density reference: [how much a yard of gravel weighs](/materials/how-much-does-a-yard-of-gravel-weigh). Coverage from tons: [how much area a ton covers](/materials/how-much-area-does-a-ton-of-gravel-cover).

## Why another 10×10 might need a different quantity

- Different **finish depth**  
- Uneven grade or soft spots  
- Different **product density** when buying by the ton  
- Optional extra-material percentage  
- Separate base and surface layers (especially driveways)
