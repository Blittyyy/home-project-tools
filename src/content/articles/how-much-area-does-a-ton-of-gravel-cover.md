**One US short ton** of gravel covers different areas at different depths.

Using the FixUpWise crushed-stone planning density of **100 lb/ft³** (Caterpillar loose crushed stone = **2,700 lb/yd³**):

| Depth | Approx. sq ft covered by **1 US short ton** |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 in | **240** |
| 2 in | **120** |
| 3 in | **80** |
| 4 in | **60** |
| 6 in | **40** |

These are **mathematical coverage examples**, not installation-depth recommendations. Change the density and every cell moves.

<aside class="planning-note" role="note">
  <p class="planning-note__title">Depth and density both matter</p>
  <p class="planning-note__body">A coverage-per-ton number is incomplete without stating how deep the layer is and which bulk density was assumed. Deeper layers cover fewer square feet per ton; denser material also covers fewer square feet per ton.</p>
</aside>

## Why depth changes coverage

The same ton is a fixed mass. Spread thinner, it covers more area; spread thicker, it covers less.

## Why density changes coverage

Heavier material (higher lb/ft³) means each ton occupies **less volume**, so it covers fewer square feet at the same depth. Compare densities in [how much a yard of gravel weighs](/materials/how-much-does-a-yard-of-gravel-weigh).

## Formula

Aligned with the [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator):

1. **Volume in cubic feet = weight in pounds ÷ bulk density in lb/ft³**  
2. **Coverage in square feet = volume in cubic feet ÷ depth in feet**

For **1 US short ton** = **2,000 lb** at density **D** lb/ft³ and depth **d** inches:

**sq ft = (2,000 ÷ D) ÷ (d ÷ 12) = (2,000 × 12) ÷ (D × d)**

At **D = 100** and **d = 2**: sq ft = 24,000 ÷ 200 = **120**.

## Worked example

**How many tons for 600 sq ft at 3 in**, same **100 lb/ft³** crushed-stone planning density? This is **geometric volume / weight** (Extra material **0%**). Set Extra material to **0%** in the [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator) to match — the default is **10%**.

- Depth = 0.25 ft  
- Volume = 600 × 0.25 = **150 cu ft** ≈ **5.56 yd³**  
- Weight = 150 × 100 = **15,000 lb** = **7.5 US short tons** before extra material  

Or from the table: 600 ÷ 80 = **7.5 tons**.

## Using the table when ordering

1. Measure area and choose depth  
2. Confirm whether the supplier’s density matches **100 lb/ft³** (or convert with their factor)  
3. Divide area by the table’s sq-ft-per-ton value — or run the calculator  
4. Add contingency only if you intend to
