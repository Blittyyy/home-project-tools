**1 cubic yard = 27 cubic feet.** Bag count is pure package math:

`bags to meet or exceed 1 yd³ = ceil(27 ÷ bag volume in cu ft)`

Exact volume equivalents before purchase rounding:

| Bag volume | 27 ÷ bag volume | Whole bags to cover ≥ 1 yd³ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5 cu ft | 18 | **18** |
| 2 cu ft | 13.5 | **14** |
| 3 cu ft | 9 | **9** |

The FixUpWise [Mulch Calculator](/calculators/mulch-calculator) compares these same **1.5 / 2 / 3 cu ft** presets (plus an optional custom bag volume). They are common U.S. package sizes — **not** every mulch product.

## Why 2 cu ft bags need 14, not 13

`27 ÷ 2 = 13.5` cubic-foot equivalents. Thirteen bags hold only 26 cu ft — short of a yard. Fourteen bags hold 28 cu ft, which meets or exceeds one cubic yard.

## Project bags vs “bags per yard”

For a real bed, compute project volume first, then divide by bag size and round up — you rarely buy exactly one yard. Example from [How Much Mulch Do I Need?](/projects/how-much-mulch-do-i-need): **10×10 at 2 in** ≈ **16.67 cu ft** → `ceil(16.67 ÷ 2) = 9` bags of 2 cu ft.

## Always read the bag

Package volume is printed on the product. If your bags are a different size, use that number (or the calculator’s custom bag field). Bulk deliveries are ordered in cubic yards; bag math is for packaged product.

Coverage of that yard still depends on depth: [How Much Area Does a Cubic Yard of Mulch Cover?](/materials/how-much-area-does-a-yard-of-mulch-cover).
