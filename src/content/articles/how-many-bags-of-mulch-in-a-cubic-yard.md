One cubic yard contains **27 cubic feet**. That equals **18 bags** at 1.5 cu ft each, **13.5 bags** at 2 cu ft each, or **9 bags** at 3 cu ft each. Because you buy whole bags, matching or exceeding one cubic yard with 2-cu-ft bags requires **14 bags**.

Bag size matters more than bag count. “10 bags of mulch” is meaningless without knowing the volume per bag.

## The 27-cubic-foot relationship

**1 yd³ = 27 ft³**

`bags to meet or exceed 1 yd³ = ceil(27 ÷ bag volume in cu ft)`

## Bag-size table

Exact volume equivalents before purchase rounding, using the FixUpWise calculator’s common U.S. package presets:

| Bag volume | 27 ÷ bag volume | Whole bags for ≥ 1 yd³ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5 cu ft | 18 | **18** |
| 2 cu ft | 13.5 | **14** |
| 3 cu ft | 9 | **9** |

Those presets are common package sizes — not every product on the shelf.

## Why 2-cu-ft bags need 14, not 13

**27 ÷ 2 = 13.5** volume-equivalents. Thirteen bags hold **26 cu ft** — short of a yard. Fourteen bags hold **28 cu ft**, which meets or exceeds one cubic yard.

## What to check on packaging

- Cubic-foot volume (or equivalent)
- Product type / mulch material
- Any note that volume is approximate when fluffed or settled

## Bags vs bulk

For a real bed, compute project volume first, then divide by bag size and round up — you rarely buy exactly one yard. Example: a [10×10 bed at 2 in](/projects/how-much-mulch-for-10x10-area) ≈ **16.67 cu ft** → **9** bags at 2 cu ft.

Bulk deliveries are ordered in cubic yards. Bag math is for packaged product. Coverage of that yard still depends on depth: [how much area a yard covers](/materials/how-much-area-does-a-yard-of-mulch-cover). Project formula: [how much mulch do I need?](/projects/how-much-mulch-do-i-need).
