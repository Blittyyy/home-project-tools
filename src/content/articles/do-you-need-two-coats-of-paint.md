**No — not always.** Coat count depends on the product instructions, the color change, the surface, primer use, and the finish you want. Treating “two coats” as a universal rule overbuys some jobs and undercovers others.

## What manufacturers signal

- Benjamin Moore’s online paint calculator **assumes two coats** for its rough estimate and excludes the ceiling.
- Some Benjamin Moore product sheets (for example Regal Select) recommend **two coats for full color development** on that product.
- Behr’s interior buying guidance notes you may need **more paint / additional coats** for porous, rough, textured, previously unpainted surfaces, or dramatic color changes.
- Sherwin-Williams notes that product requirements vary with application method and surface type or condition.

Always follow the label and technical data for the specific paint.

## Why FixUpWise defaults to 2 coats

The [Paint Calculator](/calculators/paint-calculator) starts wall coats at **2** because it is a practical estimating assumption aligned with common manufacturer calculator defaults — **not** a claim that every wall needs two coats. Change the coats field to match your plan.

## How coats change quantity

`gallons = paintable area × coats ÷ coverage`

Doubling coats doubles coated square footage at the same coverage. Purchase rounding can still jump by a full gallon.

## Factors that often push toward more than one coat

- Large color change (especially light over dark or strong hues)
- Bare drywall or porous substrates (primer may be required separately)
- Uneven previous finish or repairs that show through
- Products that specify two coats for full hide or color development
- Desire for a more uniform sheen and appearance

## Factors that can allow fewer coats

- Same or similar color over a sound, previously painted surface
- Product marketed/tested for one-coat hide in specified colors (still follow the label)
- Proper primer that improves hide before finish coats

Primer is **not** a substitute “coat of paint” in the wall-paint quantity field — estimate primer with its own coverage if you need it.
