**10 × 10 ft = 100 sq ft.**

At a **10%** planning allowance:

- **12×12** → **110** tiles  
- **12×24** → **55** tiles  
- **24×24** → **28** tiles  

“10×10 room” gives you the floor area, but not the purchase quantity until tile size and extra-material allowance are known. A common mistake is giving one universal tile count for a 100-sq-ft room.

## The 100-sq-ft room

`raw = 100 ÷ tile face area`  
`tiles to buy = ceil(raw × (1 + waste%))`

Full method: [how much tile do I need?](/projects/how-much-tile-do-i-need).

The 24×24 case shows one final ceiling: `25 × 1.10 = 27.5` → **28**. These match the [Tile Calculator](/calculators/tile-calculator).

## Extra-material comparison

Same 12×12 job (`raw = 100`):

| Allowance | Purchase tiles |
| --- | --- |
| 0% | 100 |
| 10% | 110 |
| 15% | 115 |

**10%** is a common straight-layout planning default; **15%** is often used for diagonal or more cut-heavy work. Neither is a fixed rule: [how much extra tile should you buy?](/guides/how-much-extra-tile-should-you-buy).

## Boxes depend on packaging

Convert after you have the purchase count. Carton tiles-per-box or listed coverage controls — not a universal “tiles per box” assumption. See [square feet in a box of tile](/materials/how-many-square-feet-in-a-box-of-tile).

## Why another 10×10 room may still differ

- Different tile size or format  
- Diagonal vs straight layout  
- Irregular walls, niches, or many jogs  
- Dye-lot / spare-tile preference
